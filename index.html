    Selection and scoring rules

    Geometry and bearings are tracked separately. Two independent first-check successes on different variations at the current demand unlock the next demand in that strand. Supported answers still earn practice credit. The ±1.00 rule applies to progression too; exact answers are distinguished in the record.

    Difficulty is never based on speed. Related prerequisite questions and later rechecks follow difficulty. Untested eligible variations are mixed into the route so that successful learners also meet different applications. Encountering a variation is not proof of mastery.

    This is a local device view, not a networked class dashboard or an authenticated assessment. “Independent” means first check without using the app’s help; it does not establish whether outside help was used.

    Your classroom acceptance band

    Numeric entries and calculated model answers are rounded to two decimal places. The app then compares integer hundredths. Every value within ±1.00 inclusive is accepted.

    For a model answer of 25.36, all hundredths from 24.36 to 26.36 are correct. 24.35 and 26.37 are not.

    The band uses the answer unit: ±1 mm in a millimetre box, ±1 m in a metre box, or ±1° for an angle. This can be generous for some questions. Aim for the actual two-decimal model answer, not just the band.

    Bearings use the shorter distance around a circle. North is both 000° and 360°. For a model bearing of 359.75°, 000.25° is only 0.50° away and is accepted. Enter a bearing between 000° and 360°, not extra full turns.

    This is not a real engineering tolerance, a design approval or an official assessment marking scheme.

    About this resource

    This activity develops the 3D trigonometry and bearings in the supplied worksheet. It retains the cuboid, prism, mast, survey-distance and two-leg-route skills, with consistent redrawn geometry and additional engineering applications. It contains 36 randomised question families.

    T Level connection: City & Guilds’ Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing specification, version 1.5, lists Pythagoras, triangle calculations, trigonometric functions, sine/cosine rules and vectors/coordinates under common core 4.1. Sections 5.1 and 5.2 include units and Cartesian/polar coordinates. These are the basis for the additional engineering emphasis.

    The supplied bearings and line-to-plane tasks are applied practice built on those skills. They are not presented as a complete T Level syllabus or as replicas of an awarding-body paper. The user’s particular pathway and cohort were not specified. Confirm the full teaching programme against your centre’s specification.

    Corrections to the source: perpendicular rectangular-base dimensions are combined using Pythagoras, not added as a broken-line route. Missing-edge questions use exact stated diagonal data rather than a hidden unrounded diagonal. Bearings and labels are drawn from the same data used for marking.

    Engineering contexts are simplified mathematical models. Dimensions are nominal; do not use the exercise as a fabrication drawing, survey instruction, lifting plan or structural design approval. Account for real tolerances, uncertainty and professional checks outside this classroom resource.

    There is no answer-reveal-all control. Help gives methods and different-data examples. An attempted question must be closed before its solution can be reviewed. A closed question cannot earn further credit. Standalone HTML is not tamper-proof against edited code, developer tools or altered browser data.
